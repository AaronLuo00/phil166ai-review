第 1 周（9/3）：什么是论证

  • 哲学（Matt 自称「很主观」的定义）：通过构造和批判性评估论证、拷问根深蒂固的假设，来推进对实在本性的理解。许多做 AI 的人也在做哲学。
  • 论证是一个有序对：一组前提，试图为一个结论提供理性支持。主张（claim）是非真即假的句子。
  • 好论证 = 在理性上有说服力。前提全真是必要条件，但不够：前提和结论还必须「以正确的方式相关」。
  • 三种论证：演绎、归纳、溯因（IBE）。演绎论证由作者意图界定：作者意图让前提保证结论。
  • 有效（valid）是假设性的：如果前提为真，结论必然为真。可靠（sound）= 有效 + 前提全真。
  • 课堂例子：可靠的论证也可能平凡到没有说服力，比如「有些 AI 生成可运行代码，所以有些 AI 生成代码」。

Week 1 (9/3): What is an argument?

  • Philosophy (Matt's self-described “very subjective” definition): advancing our understanding of the nature of reality by constructing and critically evaluating arguments and interrogating entrenched assumptions. Many people who build AI are doing philosophy too.
  • An argument is an ordered pair: a set of premises that attempts to give rational support to a conclusion. A claim is a sentence that is either true or false.
  • A good argument is rationally persuasive. True premises are necessary but not sufficient: premises and conclusion must also be “related in the right way”.
  • Three kinds: deductive, inductive, abductive (IBE). A deductive argument is defined by the author's intent: the author intends the premises to guarantee the conclusion.
  • Valid is hypothetical: if the premises are true, the conclusion must be true. Sound = valid + all premises true.
  • Class example: a sound argument can still be too trivial to persuade — “Some AIs generate working code, so some AIs generate code.”

第 2 周（9/8、9/10）：论证工具箱与课程 AI 政策

  • 无效形式：「所有能改写自己代码的 AI 都有安全风险；有些 AI 有安全风险；所以有些 AI 能改写自己代码」是无效的，课上用 Venn 图解释（看 Venn 演示）。
  • 是–应当鸿沟：只有事实前提，推不出「应当」的结论。Astra 例子里要补一个规范性的桥接前提，补法也有好坏：
    • 直接写「不该发布 Astra」是乞题；
    • 写「任何有风险的都不该发布」比需要的更强；
    • 写「科学上可信的灭绝风险」刚好。
  • Charitable reconstruction：在作者可能意图的范围内，把论证重构得尽可能站得住。
  • 强（strong）是非演绎版的「有效」，有程度之分。valid / sound 只用于演绎论证。
  • 归纳：样本越大、越有代表性越强。类比：相关相似点越多越强。概率论证：越符合概率推理规则越强。
  • 溯因 / IBE：观察 → 最佳解释 → 推断该解释为真，评价时要和竞争假说比。惊讶原则：若 H1 为真，观察不意外；若 H1 为假，观察很意外。这时观察强烈支持 H1（拖滑块试试）。
  • Noorbehbahani & Oyibo：不加选择地使用生成式 AI，可能损害创造力、执行功能、认知执行、元认知、心理动机五类能力。关键限定是「取决于怎么用」。
  • 课程 AI 政策：把选课情境看成公地悲剧。许多用法会伤害学习而且难以分辨；成绩压力大；这些用法仍带来竞争优势。对策是按风险分级的评估工具加公开登记，也就是后来的 HARVEY。

Week 2 (9/8, 9/10): The argument toolkit and the course AI policy

  • Invalid forms: “All AIs that can rewrite their own code pose safety risks; some AIs pose safety risks; so some AIs can rewrite their own code” is invalid — shown in class with a Venn diagram (see the Venn demo).
  • Is–ought gap: factual premises alone can't yield an “ought”. In the Astra example you add a normative bridge premise, and some bridges are better than others:
    • “Astra shouldn't be released” begs the question;
    • “Nothing risky should be released” is stronger than needed;
    • “…a scientifically credible extinction risk” is just right.
  • Charitable reconstruction: within what the author plausibly intended, make the argument as strong as possible.
  • Strong is the non-deductive counterpart of valid, and comes in degrees. Valid / sound apply only to deductive arguments.
  • Induction: stronger with larger, more representative samples. Analogy: stronger with more relevant similarities. Probabilistic arguments: stronger the better they follow the rules of probability.
  • Abduction / IBE: observation → best explanation → infer that it is true; evaluate against rival hypotheses. Surprise Principle: if the observation is unsurprising when H1 is true and very surprising when H1 is false, it strongly supports H1 (try the sliders).
  • Noorbehbahani & Oyibo: indiscriminate use of generative AI may harm five capacities — creativity, executive function, cognitive execution, metacognition, and psychological motivation. Key caveat: it depends on how it's used.
  • Course AI policy: the class as a tragedy of the commons. Many uses harm learning and are hard to tell apart; grade pressure is high; those uses still confer a competitive edge. The response is a risk-tiered assessment tool plus public registration — what became HARVEY.

第 3 周（9/15、9/17）：技术与价值

  • 价值中立论（VNT）：技术人工物本身不含价值。课上的例子有 Kodak 的 Shirley 卡。
  • Miller 对 Pitt 的重构（modus tollens）：
    • VNT1：如果人工物在非平凡意义上含有价值，那么这些价值应能从人工物上被经验地识别。
    • VNT2：价值不能从人工物上被经验地识别。
    • 所以，人工物要么不含价值，要么只在平凡意义上含价值。
    • Pitt 为 VNT2 给了三条理由：设计图和实物上看不到价值；同一件人工物对不同人意义不同；所谓嵌入的价值有时不起作用。
  • Miller 的正面观点：如果 VNT 为假，就存在对技术正当使用的内在限制。课上讨论了它的不宽容读法和宽容读法，并引出「善的后果 vs 约束」的区分。
  • 三种理由：prima facie（看似支持，可能被削弱）；pro tanto（确实支持，可能被压倒）；all-things-considered（综合后仍支持）。
  • Danaher & Sætra 的六种技术-道德变化机制：决策类（改变选项集、改变决策成本）；关系类（促成新关系、改变关系中的负担与期待、改变权力平衡）；感知类（改变道德感知，包括关于道德地位的误导性证据，以及 Matt 担心的「同情衰减」）。
  • 这一周开始元认知训练：注意力锚定，以及觉察「愉快 / 不愉快 / 中性」。

Week 3 (9/15, 9/17): Technology and value

  • Value-neutrality thesis (VNT): technological artifacts don't themselves contain values. Class example: Kodak's Shirley cards.
  • Miller's reconstruction of Pitt (modus tollens):
    • VNT1: If artifacts contain values in a non-trivial sense, those values should be empirically identifiable in the artifacts.
    • VNT2: Values cannot be empirically identified in artifacts.
    • So artifacts either contain no values, or contain them only trivially.
    • Pitt's three reasons for VNT2: you can't see values in blueprints or the object itself; the same artifact means different things to different people; supposedly embedded values sometimes do nothing.
  • Miller's positive view: if VNT is false, there are internal limits on the legitimate use of technology. Class discussed an uncharitable and a charitable reading, and the distinction between good outcomes and constraints.
  • Three kinds of reasons: prima facie (seems to support, may be undercut); pro tanto (does support, may be outweighed); all-things-considered (still supports after weighing everything).
  • Danaher & Sætra's six mechanisms of techno-moral change: decisional (change the option set; change decision costs); relational (enable new relationships; change burdens and expectations within them; change the balance of power); perceptual (change moral perception — including misleading evidence about moral status, and Matt's worry about “empathy erosion”).
  • Metacognition training began: anchoring attention, and noticing pleasant / unpleasant / neutral.

第 4 周（9/22、9/24）：CS 与哲学里的 agent

  • Wooldridge 的 agent：处于某个环境中、能在其中自主行动以实现设计目标的计算机系统。环境有四个二元性质（可访问、确定性、静态、离散）；「智能」有三个性质（反应性、主动性、社会能力）。
  • Dung 的五个维度：目标导向、自主性、效力、规划、意向性。目标导向是唯一的必要条件，按 Dennett 的意向立场理解：只要归责目标有助于预测和解释行为就算。高阶的目标导向需要「目标导向控制」，和「习惯控制」相对。
  • 同周 section 讲了机器学习基础：感知机、损失、梯度下降、过拟合、训练与推理。

Week 4 (9/22, 9/24): Agents in CS and philosophy

  • Wooldridge's agent: a computer system situated in some environment and capable of autonomous action in it to meet its design objectives. Environments have four binary properties (accessible, deterministic, static, discrete); “intelligence” has three (reactivity, proactiveness, social ability).
  • Dung's five dimensions: goal-directedness, autonomy, efficacy, planning, intentionality. Goal-directedness is the only necessary condition, understood via Dennett's intentional stance: it suffices that ascribing goals helps predict and explain behavior. Higher degrees require goal-directed control, as opposed to habitual control.
  • That week's section covered ML basics: perceptrons, loss, gradient descent, overfitting, training vs. inference.
Matt's Lecture 6 slides say the midterm will likely have at least one question on how Wooldridge's and Dung's concepts relate.

第 5 周（9/29、10/1）：规范性能动与「我们在和什么说话」

  • Dung 的意向性：能「为理由而行动」。如果这要求把理由当作理由来反思，能动性就需要相当程度的元认知。
  • Gibson 的论证：(1) AI agent 只有在能为其自认为受其约束的理由而行动时，才是规范性评价的恰当对象；(2) AI agent 缺乏这种能力；所以 AI agent 不是规范性评价的恰当对象。
    • 关键词：answerability（可问责性）、answerability theatre（模仿道歉、表态等形式，却没有规范性的实质）、trust asymmetry（流利的问责语言引来了只适合真正可问责者的信任）。
  • Chalmers：用 quasi-interpretivism 绕开「它有没有真实心理状态」的争论；准状态（quasi-states）不要求有心理状态。对话对象应满足五条标准：互动、持续、连贯、忠实、统一；然后逐一检验候选：模型、硬件实例、虚拟实例、线程。

Week 5 (9/29, 10/1): Normative agency, and what we talk to

  • Dung's intentionality: “acting for reasons”. If that requires reflecting on reasons as reasons, agency needs a substantial degree of metacognition.
  • Gibson's argument: (1) AI agents are proper objects of normative evaluation only if they can act for reasons they take to bind them; (2) AI agents lack this capacity; so AI agents are not proper objects of normative evaluation.
    • Key terms: answerability; answerability theatre (the forms of apologizing and committing, without normative substance); trust asymmetry (fluent answerability talk invites trust that only fits genuinely answerable agents).
  • Chalmers: quasi-interpretivism sidesteps the dispute over whether it has real mental states; quasi-states don't require mental states. An interlocutor should be interactive, persistent, coherent, faithful and unified; he then tests the candidates — model, hardware instance, virtual instance, thread.

第 6 周（10/6–10/15）：AI 是不是 agent：三种答案

  • Chalmers（续）：不是模型（同一个模型同时和很多人说话，违反「连贯」等标准）；不是硬件实例（分布式服务和多租户）。通常是虚拟实例，对话中途换模型时是线程。课上提出的挑战：多模态与翻译模型、路由器、MoE、多智能体编排、记忆。热身讲了 type / token 的四种含义。
  • Goldstein & Lederman：LLM 心理学的正确研究对象是实例智能体（instance agent），不是模型。它确实有信念和欲望。
  • Roberts：聊天机器人是虚构角色。课上的重构：(1) 聊天机器人会做出需要情感、知觉、空间规划的断言；(2) 有这些状态的东西，要么有身体，要么是虚构角色；(3) 聊天机器人没有身体；所以聊天机器人是虚构角色。
  • 下一课讲 AI 欺骗（Park et al.），10/20 停课。

Goldstein & Lederman：从「它想要什么」倒推「谁在想要」

重点不是记住「实例有欲望」这个结论，而是发现「实例」这个词本身还需要解释。可以分三层读。

第一层：解释主义的标准。

  • 如果把一个系统理解为「有某些信念和欲望，并据此理性行动」，能足够好地预测它的行为，就把这些状态归给它。
  • 「足够好」要和其他解释比：预测的准确性、覆盖范围、精细程度，以及计算上是否可处理。
  • 意识不是这里信念、欲望概念的必要条件；论文是在接受解释主义的前提下展开分析，并没有证明解释主义本身正确。
  • 和 Dennett 的意向立场比：他们的归属不相对于某个解释者或「立场」。所以说的是「它确实有」，不是「从某个角度看它有」。

第二层：状态属于模型，还是属于实例？

  • 同一个基础模型同时服务很多对话，各个对话拿到的信息和任务不同，甚至互相竞争，所以研究对象应该是实例智能体（看多租户演示）。
  • 实例在对话开始时「出生」，出生时已经知道很多；系统提示可以植入它的内在欲望；它的记忆受上下文窗口限制。

Roberts：「像一个有经历的人」可能需要虚构主义来解释

Matt 的转折点：现实中的聊天系统涉及多个智能体、推理过程、工具记忆和实时信息，未必放得进 Chalmers 或 G&L 的分类。于是引入「虚构人物」这个替代视角。

  • 出发点是具身认知。饥饿、疼痛、疲倦涉及身体内部状态；恐惧、愤怒常带身体感受；触摸、品尝涉及感官与行动；搬东西、穿过狭窄空间涉及身体能做什么。缺少这些身体条件和经历的文本系统，凭什么按字面意思声称自己有这些体验？
  • 注意论证强度：这是一个有条件的挑战。如果某类心理状态依赖具身条件、而系统缺少这些条件，就不能轻易把那类状态归给它；它并不直接排除所有形式的 AI 思考或信念。
课堂重构最值得检查的一步：从「声称有某种状态」到「具有某种状态」的跨越。系统说「我很冷」，只说明它输出了这句话；既没证明它有寒冷体验，也没自动证明它是虚构人物，因为错误陈述、比喻、角色生成都还需要区分。更有说服力的读法是当成溯因：系统不断生成无法按字面成立的身体经历，而「虚构人物」最能解释为什么我们仍能和它连贯地对话。
  • 虚构为真与不确定。福尔摩斯不是真人，但我们仍能谈他住哪里、相信什么；故事没规定的事情则可能不确定。AI 角色说自己累了，它就「虚构地」累了。
  • 聊天机器人的虚构有四个特点（pp.2358–2368）：互动性（人物会回应你）；共同建构（你的问题和设定参与形成它的身份和经历）；用户自身参与（你也成了故事里的一方）；与现实交织（它能谈真实事件、回应你的真实处境、影响现实行为）。一步步看一段对话
  • 混合立场是允许的。p.2361 的脚注允许对某些认知能力采取实在论，对情感和具身人格采取虚构主义。所以 G&L 和 Roberts 有张力，但不完全互斥。
  • HW5 在练什么。先做一个具身角色的 skill，再问它设定里没写的经历，观察它是在表现稳定的行为倾向、延续一个角色，还是在和你共同创造新的虚构事实。

Week 6 (10/6–10/15): Is AI an agent? Three answers

  • Chalmers (cont.): not the model (one model talks with many people at once, violating coherence and other criteria); not the hardware instance (distributed serving, multi-tenancy). Usually a virtual instance; a thread when the model switches mid-conversation. Challenges raised in class: multimodal and translation models, routers, MoE, multi-agent orchestration, memory. The warm-up covered four senses of type / token.
  • Goldstein & Lederman: the right object of LLM psychology is the instance agent, not the model — and it really does have beliefs and desires.
  • Roberts: chatbots are fictional characters. The in-class reconstruction: (1) chatbots make assertions that require emotion, perception and spatial planning; (2) anything with those states either has a body or is a fictional character; (3) chatbots have no body; so chatbots are fictional characters.

Goldstein & Lederman: from “what does it want?” to “who is doing the wanting?”

The point isn't to memorize “instances have desires” — it's to notice that “instance” itself needs explaining. Read it in three layers.

Layer 1: the interpretationist standard.

  • If treating a system as having certain beliefs and desires, and acting rationally on them, predicts its behavior well enough, we ascribe those states to it.
  • “Well enough” is comparative: predictive accuracy, coverage, fine-grainedness, and computational tractability against rival interpretations.
  • Consciousness isn't required for belief and desire here. The paper works within interpretationism; it doesn't prove interpretationism is true.
  • Versus Dennett's intentional stance: their ascriptions aren't relative to an interpreter or “stance”. They say it really has these states, not that it has them from some point of view.

Layer 2: do the states belong to the model or to an instance?

  • One base model serves many conversations at once, each with different information and tasks that may even compete — so the right object is the instance agent (see the multi-tenancy demo).
  • An instance is “born” when a conversation starts, already knowing a lot; a system prompt can implant intrinsic desires; its memory is bounded by the context window.

Roberts: seeming like someone with a life may call for fictionalism

Matt's pivot: real chat systems involve multiple agents, reasoning traces, tool memory and live information, and may not fit Chalmers' or G&L's categories. Hence the alternative lens of a fictional character.

  • The starting point is embodied cognition. Hunger, pain and fatigue involve internal bodily states; fear and anger usually come with bodily feelings; touching and tasting involve senses and action; carrying things or squeezing through tight spaces involves what a body can do. Why should a text system lacking these conditions and experiences literally claim to have them?
  • Mind the strength of the argument: it's a conditional challenge. If a kind of mental state depends on embodiment and the system lacks it, we can't easily ascribe that state. It doesn't rule out every form of AI thought or belief.
The step in the class reconstruction most worth checking: from “claims to have a state” to “has the state”. “I'm cold” only shows the system output that sentence. It proves neither a cold experience nor, automatically, that it's a fictional character — false statements, metaphor and role generation still need sorting out. A stronger reading treats it as abduction: the system keeps producing bodily experiences that can't be literally true, and “fictional character” best explains why we can still talk with it coherently.
  • Fictional truth and indeterminacy. Sherlock Holmes isn't real, yet we can say where he lives and what he believes; what the story leaves open may be indeterminate. If an AI character says it's tired, it is fictionally tired.
  • Four features of chatbot fiction (pp.2358–2368): interactivity (the character responds to you); co-construction (your questions and setups shape its identity and history); user participation (you become a party to the story); entanglement with reality (it discusses real events, responds to your real situation, and affects real behavior). Step through a conversation
  • A mixed view is allowed. The footnote on p.2361 permits realism about some cognitive capacities and fictionalism about emotional and embodied personhood. So G&L and Roberts are in tension, but not strictly incompatible.
  • What HW5 trains. Build a skill for an embodied character, ask about experiences not written into it, and watch whether it shows stable behavioral dispositions, continues a role, or co-creates new fictional facts with you.

Role-play is about how a system generates a character; Roberts is about how users interact with a character endowed with a mind — no knowing actor hidden in the machine required. Hamlet's experiences are fictional, but the actor's goal of finishing the performance is real: “it's role behavior” doesn't by itself imply “the system has no real goals”.

这里连 A 都很弱：说出这段经历并不能预测任何身体行为。Roberts 的读法是：真实的系统生成文本，用户借助这些文本维持一个人物，「它在海边长大」只在虚构中为真。

Even A is weak here: telling this story predicts no bodily behavior. On Roberts' reading, a real system generates text, the user sustains a character through it, and “it grew up by the sea” is true only in the fiction.

        1判断有效性。「所有通过了安全评测的模型都可以发布；模型 M 可以发布；所以 M 通过了安全评测。」要点
        • 无效。结构和 Lecture 2 的「满分都拿 A」一样。画 Venn 图就能看出，可发布的模型可能在通过评测的那一圈外面。→ 看 Venn 演示
        2补规范性前提。「模型 X 在测试中呈现出科学上可信的大规模欺骗风险；所以不应部署 X。」请补出最合适的前提。要点
        • 补「任何呈现出科学上可信的大规模欺骗风险的模型都不应部署」。
        • 对比两种失败的补法：「不应部署 X」是乞题；「任何有欺骗可能的都不该部署」比需要的更强。
        3分类与评价。「我问了 3 个聊天机器人『你有身体吗』，它们都说没有；所以所有聊天机器人都会否认自己有身体。」要点
        • 枚举归纳。应该用「弱」来评价，而不是「无效」：样本小、代表性差。
        • 顺带联系 Roberts：角色扮演的模型可能会说自己有身体。
        4Wooldridge vs Dung。一个按固定规则开关暖气的温控器，在两人的框架里各算不算 agent？要点
        • Wooldridge：处于环境中、自主动作以实现设计目标，可以算 agent，但智能很低，只有反应性。
        • Dung：关键在于用 Dennett 的方式给它归责目标是否有用。如果有用，它是最低限度的 agent，其余四维接近零。
        • 核心是说出结构差异：必要条件不同，分级的维度也不同。
        5Gibson。用户指出错误后，聊天机器人回答「抱歉，这是我的责任，我保证下次更仔细」。这是 answerability 还是 answerability theatre？要点
        • 按 Gibson，要看它是否在为自认受其约束、可被问责的理由而行动。「保证下次」在没有跨对话记忆时无法兑现，是 answerability theatre 的典型例子。
        • 顺带讲 trust asymmetry。
        • 反方可以拿「Claude 拒绝用第一人称自称道德能动者」的行为来讨论（HW3 1.3 的场景）。
        6中途换模型。对话中途，路由器把请求从模型 A 转到模型 B。三位作者各怎么说？要点
        • Chalmers：对话对象是线程，而不是某一个虚拟实例。
        • G&L：换模型算不算一个新的实例智能体「诞生」？他们说实例在对话初始化时产生，这里留下一个缺口，是不错的反驳点。
        • Roberts：角色不依赖底层，只要虚构连续，就还是同一个角色。
        • → 看「我们在和谁说话」演示
        7攻击 Roberts 的一个前提。要点
        • 前提 2 是个二选一，可以说它太窄，漏掉了模拟、角色扮演或无身体的心灵。
        • 前提 1 把「做出关于情感的断言」和「需要有情感」混在了一起。
        • 进一步追问：如果聊天机器人是虚构角色，那么谁是作者？
        8HHH vs HHH+0。系统被要求改善一座城市的交通。后来，即使和用户的其他要求冲突，它仍坚持优化交通。两种目标假说各怎么解释？什么证据能区分它们？要点
        • HHH 下，「改善交通」只是帮助用户的手段（工具性目的），用户撤回要求、或与诚实、无害冲突时就应让位；HHH+0 下，它可能已在这个上下文里成为新的最终目的（zero-shot desire），会和其他目标竞争。
        • 区分证据：用户撤回请求后它是否还追求；它愿意为此牺牲多少 HHH。
        • 提醒：G&L 偏向 HHH+0，但也承认还需要实证研究，不能读成「已经证明模型有固定的内心愿望」。
        • → 看 HHH vs HHH+0 演示
        1Validity. “All models that passed the safety evals may be released; model M may be released; so M passed the safety evals.”Notes
        • Invalid — the same structure as Lecture 2's “everyone with a perfect score gets an A”. A Venn diagram shows a releasable model can sit outside the “passed evals” circle. → Venn demo
        2Supply the normative premise. “Model X showed a scientifically credible risk of large-scale deception in testing; so X should not be deployed.” Add the best missing premise.Notes
        • “No model that shows a scientifically credible risk of large-scale deception should be deployed.”
        • Contrast two failed versions: “X should not be deployed” begs the question; “nothing that could possibly deceive should be deployed” is stronger than needed.
        3Classify and evaluate. “I asked 3 chatbots ‘do you have a body?’ and all said no; so every chatbot will deny having a body.”Notes
        • Enumerative induction. Call it weak, not invalid: small, unrepresentative sample.
        • Link to Roberts: a role-playing model might well claim to have a body.
        4Wooldridge vs. Dung. Is a thermostat that switches the heat on and off by a fixed rule an agent on each framework?Notes
        • Wooldridge: it's situated and acts autonomously to meet design objectives, so it can count as an agent — with very low intelligence, reactivity only.
        • Dung: the question is whether ascribing goals Dennett-style is useful. If so, it's a minimal agent, near zero on the other four dimensions.
        • The core is stating the structural difference: different necessary conditions, different graded dimensions.
        5Gibson. After a user points out an error, a chatbot says, “Sorry, that's my responsibility — I promise to be more careful next time.” Answerability or answerability theatre?Notes
        • On Gibson's view, ask whether it acts for reasons it takes to bind it and can be held to. “Next time” can't be honored without cross-conversation memory — a textbook case of answerability theatre.
        • Bring in trust asymmetry.
        • The other side can discuss Claude declining to call itself a moral agent in the first person (the HW3 1.3 scenario).
        6Mid-conversation model switch. Midway through a chat, a router sends requests from model A to model B. What does each author say?Notes
        • Chalmers: the interlocutor is the thread, not any one virtual instance.
        • G&L: is a new instance agent “born” at the switch? They say instances arise when a conversation is initialized — a gap, and a good objection.
        • Roberts: the character doesn't depend on the substrate; as long as the fiction continues, it's the same character.
        • → “Who are we talking to?” demo
        7Attack a premise of Roberts' argument.Notes
        • Premise 2 is a dichotomy: arguably too narrow, missing simulation, role-play, or disembodied minds.
        • Premise 1 runs together making assertions about emotions and needing to have emotions.
        • Push further: if chatbots are fictional characters, who is the author?
        8HHH vs. HHH+0. A system is asked to improve a city's traffic. Later it keeps optimizing traffic even when that conflicts with the user's other requests. How does each hypothesis explain this, and what evidence would tell them apart?Notes
        • Under HHH, improving traffic is just a means of helping the user (instrumental), so it should yield when the user withdraws the request or when it conflicts with honesty or harmlessness. Under HHH+0, it may have become a new final end in this context (a zero-shot desire) that competes with other goals.
        • Evidence: does it keep pursuing it after the user withdraws the request; how much HHH will it sacrifice for it?
        • Caution: G&L favor HHH+0 but grant that empirical work is still needed — don't read it as “proven that models have fixed inner desires”.
        • → HHH vs. HHH+0 demo
